A bigger wardrobe is not the same as a better wardrobe. Start with one textured overshirt, one solid tee and one dark straight-fit trouser. Wear the tee alone, layer the overshirt, switch the tuck, roll the sleeves, or swap in clean sneakers. That is already a week of useful combinations. Build systems before you buy more. ## Day 2: One overshirt. Three small fit moves in 10 seconds.

One overshirt, three small styling moves. First, wear it open so the cream tee stays visible and the layer feels relaxed. Second, button it lightly to create a cleaner front line without pulling the fabric. Third, check the collar, cuff and hem, then stop when the layer sits cleanly. ## Day 5: A realistic fit check, from shoulder to shoe.

Use this as a mirror checklist, not a before-and-after. First, check that the sleeve seam sits near the shoulder and the tee lies smoothly without pulling. Second, check that the tee hem finishes close to the waistband with enough length to move and no excess bunching. Third, follow the trouser line down to a clean ankle and shoe. Read the whole silhouette before buying another piece. Save the three checks and test them on an outfit you already own. Follow @budgetfitzz for practical fit guidance.

## Day 6: One sneaker budget? Rank by use, not hype.

If you can buy one pair, rank the job before the hype. Neutral court sneaker: best for maximum outfit compatibility. Retro runner: best when walking comfort matters most. Chunky sneaker: strongest statement, but the least flexible with smart-casual trousers. Score your week across college or office, walking time and dress code before choosing. ## Day 7: Before Sunday checkout: pass this 5-point audit.

Before you check out, ask five things. Can I build three outfits with pieces I own? Does it fit my body now? Does it solve a real wardrobe gap? Will I wear it at least twice a month? Would I still want it without the discount label? Four yes answers means consider it. Three or fewer means wait 48 hours. Save the audit and comment the rule that saves you the most money.
